# Human–Computer Interaction

*A living textbook for the era of AI, wearables and brain interfaces.*

Most HCI textbooks were written for a world of desktops, menus and mice. That world still matters, but
people now talk to language models, wear sensors, wave at cameras, and, in the lab and increasingly in
the clinic, control machines with their brain activity. This book is written **gradually and in the open**
to cover both the lasting foundations of HCI and the new ways humans and computers meet.

## Who is this for?

- **Primary audience:** bachelor students in computer science, cognitive science, psychology, design and
  related fields. No prior HCI knowledge is assumed.
- **Secondary audience:** anyone curious about where interfaces are heading, from keyboards to
  brain–computer interfaces (BCI).

## How the book is organised

| Part | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| I. Foundations | What do *human*, *computer* and *interaction* actually mean? |
| II. Ways of interacting | Keyboard, touch, voice, gesture, gaze, haptics, AR/VR: what is each good (and bad) at? |
| III. Designing & evaluating | How do we build interfaces that people can use, and *know* they can? |
| IV. Interacting with AI | What changes when the computer learns, guesses and talks back? |
| V. Brain–machine & brain–computer interfaces | How can neural activity become a control signal, and at what cost? |
| VI. The road ahead | Where is this going, and who decides? |

## Recurring ideas

Three questions come back in every chapter:

1. **Which human capability does this interaction rely on?** (Perception, memory, motor control, attention…)
2. **What does the computer need to sense, infer or decide?**
3. **Is it as simple as it can be?** Simplicity is a merit, not a limitation.
